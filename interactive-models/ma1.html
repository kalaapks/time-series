Explainer

To see the development of the detailed theory behind MA(1) processes, click "Time Series Analysis for Quantitative Finance" and explore Chapter 1. To see the codes behind this window, click the "/." button at "MA(1) Interactive window".

Behaviour when θ is close to 1 vs when it is cose to -1.

To see the difference first switch on the Successive Trajectory Vectors; may be increase the noise and sample size too so that trajectories are clearly visible.
You will see that when θ is close to 1, the successive realizations are in the adjacent quadrant (that is tendancy to stay close) whereas when θ is close to -1, we see the tendancy to take values in opposite quadrant (the tendancy to take similar values but with a negative sign).
This behaviour is also seen in AR(1) processes. The difference comes down to the limiting behaviour of the sample points.

Limiting behaviour for AR(1).

The formulae for variance tells us that as θ goes to 1 or -1, we the samples tend to spread by σ/√2 amount. This is finite as opposed to what we see in AR(1) processes.
The Covariance ellipse gets aligned with the sp(1,1) because of the fact that Covariance matrices are symmetric matrices and hence has eigen vectors (1,1) & (-1,1) which serves as the major and minor axes for the ellipse depending on the value of θ. In particular, since this is due to the property of symmetric matrices, this alignment is supposed to show up in AR(1) processes too.

We have the result that for |θ| < 1 , there exists AR(∞) representation for MA(1).

What does this mean? If we look at one of these plots where |θ|<1 , then this is supposed to look like AR(∞) process? We have seen AR(1) and AR(2). How do they geometrically connect to MA(1)? The answer comes down to the fact that we are only looking at Yt and Yt-1 whereas we need to look at all of the past to get the actual picture of AR(∞).
